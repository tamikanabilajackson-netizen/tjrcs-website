A mother called me on a Tuesday evening, her voice careful, like she had practiced what she was going to say and was still deciding whether to say it.

"We've tried a lot of things," she said. "I just don't want this to be one more thing that doesn't work."

The details change every time I hear some version of that sentence. The weight underneath it rarely does.

Behind it is usually a list. Programs tried. Specialists seen. Money spent. Hope spent too, which costs more and rebuilds slower.

I don't rush past that list when a parent brings it up. I ask what was tried, what helped even a little, what didn't. Not because I need the history to build the program. The structure of Build & Launch doesn't change based on what came before. I ask because a parent who has been let down needs to know the person on the other end of the phone actually heard the weight of it.

Then come the real questions underneath the big one.

Is this covered by insurance? It isn't. Build & Launch is private pay, not a clinical service, and OHIP doesn't touch it. I say that plainly and early, because a parent deciding whether to trust me deserves a straight answer before a warm one.

What if my young adult doesn't want to talk to a stranger for an hour? Most don't, at first. That's expected, not a problem to solve before we start. The structure is built for exactly that. Same time, same person, same format, every week, so trust has somewhere predictable to grow instead of being asked to appear on command.

What if this doesn't work either? I can't promise an outcome. Nobody honest can. What I can promise is that the four months are built around what your young adult is already good at, not around fixing what's wrong with them, and that tends to land differently than whatever came before.

That mother asked me one more question before we hung up, quieter than the rest.

"What if he's just not ready?"

I told her the truth. Some young adults aren't, and that's allowed. There's no penalty for starting later. No version of this where waiting means losing a chance for good. The invitation stays open.

Her son enrolled a couple of months after that call, once he said he wanted to.

If you're holding a list like hers, tried things, worn hope, a question you haven't said out loud yet, I would rather you ask me directly than keep carrying it alone. Fill out the inquiry form, or come to a weekly Instagram Live session. I'll answer the real question, not just the one you lead with.
